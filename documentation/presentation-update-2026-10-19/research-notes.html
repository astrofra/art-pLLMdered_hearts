From model replacement to situated mediation

Research notes for the English presentation, 19 October 2026. The central question is how a contemporary commentary can build a pertinent transition from Plundered Hearts to Amy Briggs's retrospective testimony. Faster generation, varied wording and a greater number of clips are useful observations, but none independently measures that transition's interpretive value.

1. The initial role change remains the main artistic decision

The first prototype asked Ministral 3:14B to play the game. Its failed commands, repeated attempts and growing action history belong to that historical experiment. The current installation instead advances through a scripted walkthrough. Gemma 4 E4B reads completed scenes and produces a situated commentary; it does not decide moves. The original slides and examples remain labelled as historical evidence, rather than being retrospectively attributed to Gemma.

2. Choosing a local commentator

We compared Ministral 3:14B, Qwen 3.5 4B and Gemma 4 E4B on twelve fixed scenes, two rounds each, in English. In that first protocol the models were preloaded, reasoning was disabled, the output cap was 256 tokens and the context window was 4,096 tokens. 3. What was repeating, and what we changed

Listening to the installation revealed recurring explanations about hardware constraints, memory limits and commercial pressure. Structured session logs then exposed the full chain: game passage, prompt, prior comments and generated reading. The model was often supplied with ready-made themes and encouraged to apply the interview to every scene. These observations motivated changes to the task itself.

4. Reasoning, output budgets and temperature

Early reasoning tests changed the prose more than the recurring themes. A later small anti-repetition test, with previous comments supplied, found more specific fresh details in several reasoning-enabled responses; it also increased latency. That justified another practical trial, not a universal claim that reasoning improves artistic relevance. A larger output ceiling does not require the model to generate that many tokens: actual stopping, prompt caching and runtime conditions determine the measured duration.

The production ceiling is now 2,560 tokens, covering reasoning plus visible output, within a configured context of 4,096 tokens. The requested visible answer remains two sentences and no more than 90 words. Shorter 512/768-token reasoning budgets had produced empty or truncated responses in a small probe. Enabling reasoning does not make this an autonomous tool-using agent: the script still controls the game and archive retrieval.

The subsequent temperature/context experiment produced 96 screened answers (four references × three temperatures × four scenes × two seeds), then five rolling replays of 55 buffers: 371 answers overall. The chosen reference has eight compact factual entries totalling 136 words, at temperature 0.3. The original 752-word reference, a 230-word prose version and a 255-word thematic version remain available. The compact reference's observed median in that replay was 4.84 s, versus 7.05 s for the original at 0.3. Higher temperatures, 0.6 and 0.9, did not consistently improve the assistant's qualitative reading. Occasional errors remained in every condition.

Sources: initial reasoning study, small memory/reasoning probe, budget probe, 371-response experiment. The verdict was not blinded or assessed by an independent panel.

5. “Lost in the middle” and fine-tuning: precise terms

Liu et al., Lost in the Middle (TACL, 2024) report sensitivity to the position of relevant information in long contexts, on their models and retrieval tasks. This motivates our question about the interview summary's structure. It does not diagnose the cause of Gemma's recurring themes in this installation.

We did not move identical facts between controlled positions. The compact version also changes content and length; it is therefore impossible to isolate a pure list-format or attention-position effect. No interview-fact order was randomised. The current prompt has substantial context margin in the new measured run. More available tokens would not by itself remove thematic anchoring.

No model weights were trained or fine-tuned. Our interventions concern model selection, prompts, supplied context, bounded memory, sampling and scheduling. Embedding the transcript is indexing, not training. A future fine-tuning project would require a curated set of judged game–comment–clip examples and held-out evaluation; it would be premature to present it as a demonstrated solution here.

They are close, not bit-identical, and this does not recover the original catalogue's complete generation provenance. The production catalogue was not overwritten. Comparing local and broader views

The follow-up interactive comparison jointly fits the same frozen vectors with PCA, t-SNE and UMAP. Thirteen recorded fits cover PCA plus three neighbourhood scales and two seeds for each non-linear method. No new commentary or embeddings were generated. The page preserves the same point IDs and colours, links selections across the three maps, and exposes the unchanged full-space ranking. Family focus zooms the existing coordinates without refitting. Parameters and package versions are saved in the comparison data.

The original-space mean cosines are 0.325 within comments, 0.439 within transcripts and 0.126 across the two families. The separation therefore precedes projection. Possible contributions from topic, style or length remain hypotheses. A third PCA axis raises retained variance only from 21.5% to 25.0%. The non-linear maps preserve more local neighbours, but can represent cross-family neighbours less faithfully: at the displayed defaults, the visually nearest clip matches the original-space top clip for 19/55 comments in PCA, 1/55 in t-SNE and 13/55 in UMAP. Those counts describe map fidelity, not the installation's retrieval accuracy or artistic relevance.

8. What remains unresolved about the game/video link

The new prompt's scene-first approach removes many forced documentary explanations, but it can also weaken the explicit connection to Briggs. In the new run, the first comment about a captive's emptied room selected “Remembering Wheeler Street and Cambridge Park” at a cosine near 0.2693, slightly ahead of “Plundered Hearts: Design and Memory Limits” near 0.2672. This is a real recorded transition, not a fabricated example. Whether that association helps an audience is still a question for a human reader of both texts.

Useful next experiments would rate complete game–comment–clip triples with a shared human rubric, compare smaller coherent subtitle segments with the existing full-clip vectors, and test embedding models while holding commentary constant. A position experiment should keep facts and other inputs identical. Diversity should remain a separate measure: more clips or fewer repeated words do not automatically mean a more pertinent documentary relationship.
